Replacing a transformer during a planned shutdown is not simply a like-for-like purchasing exercise. Before the outage starts, the owner and supplier should establish whether the replacement can physically arrive, connect, be tested, and be released under the approved switching and commissioning plan. The practical answer is a replacement-outage information pack: it connects the proposed transformer to the existing single-line diagram, civil layout, cable or bus interface, lifting route, test plan, and release authority.

This guide is for operating factories, EPC teams, utilities, and facility owners replacing an oil-immersed or dry-type transformer in an existing electrical system. It helps buyers ask for the right documents and expose interface risk before the shutdown window is committed. It is not a switching procedure, lifting plan, protection study, site-test instruction, or authorization to work on energized equipment. Those decisions remain with the asset owner, qualified electrical and lifting personnel, the responsible engineer, utility, and applicable local authority.

## Start with the outage boundary, not the new transformer catalogue

The first question is not “what rating can be supplied?” It is “what equipment and work must be isolated, removed, connected, tested, and handed back during this specific window?” A replacement with the same kVA label may still have different dimensions, mass, terminal elevations, cable-box arrangement, tap position range, vector group, cooling auxiliaries, control terminals, or transport condition.

Build the pack from the current approved record, then mark unknowns. At minimum, include the latest single-line diagram, transformer nameplate record, interface drawings, photographs of accessible terminations, cable schedules, equipment layout, existing protection and control documents, and the required outage duration. Where drawings conflict with site conditions, treat the conflict as an engineering hold point rather than allowing a supplier quotation to silently choose the interpretation.

The electrical basis also needs to remain traceable. A new unit's voltage ratio, vector group, neutral arrangement, taps, impedance where specified, insulation coordination, and applicable standard basis must be checked against the system—not inferred from the old nameplate alone. Our [vector-group RFQ guide](/resources/insights/transformer-vector-group-rfq/) and [insulation-level checklist](/resources/insights/transformer-insulation-level-rfq/) explain two interfaces that may need formal review.

## The replacement-outage data pack

Use one controlled register. Each line should name the source revision, owner, required date, and status: confirmed, pending, differs from existing, or not in supplier scope.

| Workstream | Confirm before placing the order | Do not assume |
| --- | --- | --- |
| System and isolation | Single-line diagram, equipment tags, normal and temporary operating arrangement, isolation points, utility conditions, and shutdown owner. | That a transformer can be isolated without changing the approved operating plan or affecting another load. |
| Electrical match | Voltage ratio, frequency, vector group, tap requirement, neutral treatment, terminal duty, protection and metering interfaces, and any project-specified impedance or withstand evidence. | That an equal kVA nameplate makes a unit electrically interchangeable. |
| Physical fit | Approved outline, shipping dimensions, mass, centre of gravity, base footprint, terminal orientation, cable-entry direction, clearances, and removal path. | That the existing plinth, doors, crane route, cable slack, or busduct will fit the replacement. |
| Site scope | Who removes the old unit, disconnects and reconnects cables or bus, makes joints, handles oil where applicable, supplies temporary power, and restores finishes or fire barriers. | That “delivery” includes rigging, disconnection, oil work, disposal, cable work, or civil modification. |
| Controls and protection | Auxiliary voltage, alarm/trip point list, terminal drawings, relay settings responsibility, CT/VT interfaces where applicable, interlocks, and functional-test boundary. | That existing relay settings, alarm logic, or control wiring can be reused without review. |
| Verification and release | Required factory records, site inspections, test plan, acceptance criteria, punch-list process, switching order, and sign-off authority. | That factory tests alone release an installed transformer for energization. |

The final requirements depend on the project document set and the applicable market. IEC 60076-1 is a general power-transformer reference within its stated scope and includes subjects such as transport and functional specification; it does not supply a universal outage plan. [IEEE C57.93](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/C57.93/11642/) provides installation and maintenance guidance for liquid-immersed transformers within its scope. [ANSI/NETA ECS](https://www.netaworld.org/standards/ansi-neta-ecs) provides a commissioning-specification framework for electrical power equipment and systems. Confirm the applicable edition, local code, utility requirements, supplier instructions, and project acceptance process rather than treating any one document as globally decisive.

## Separate a field survey from a supplier drawing

A supplier's outline drawing describes the offered equipment. A field survey describes the route and interfaces it must meet. Both are necessary, and neither should substitute for the other.

For the survey, record the accessible dimensions and constraints that affect removal and installation: equipment-room openings, turning points, slab or platform limitations, overhead obstructions, lifting access, cable trench cover capacity, busduct location, ventilation openings, fire barriers, drainage or oil-containment interfaces, and the condition of the existing base. Measurements and lifting decisions must be taken by competent project personnel under the site rules. Do not ask a remote drawing review to certify a route it has not verified.

For the offered unit, request the controlled outline and interface drawing early—not after production is complete. It should identify the relevant base dimensions, mass and centre of gravity, terminal positions, cable box or bus connection, earthing points, radiators or fans where supplied, accessory boxes, and transport/shipping configuration. The [foundation and mounting RFQ checklist](/resources/insights/transformer-foundation-mounting-rfq/) and [cable-box RFQ guide](/resources/insights/transformer-cable-box-rfq/) help turn those items into reviewable questions.

If the replacement needs a modified plinth, new supports, busduct transition, cable extension, or altered protection/control arrangement, make it a defined engineering scope with drawings, approvals, owner, and schedule effect. It is not a small installation detail to settle inside the outage.

## Hold points that protect the shutdown window

An outage plan is most useful when it prevents late discovery. The following hold points can be assigned to a project schedule; the actual sequence and acceptance authority must follow the approved project plan.

1. **Before award:** close the system-data, interface, physical-fit, and scope questions in the table. Record every accepted departure from the existing equipment or project requirement.
2. **Before manufacture release:** approve the data sheet, outline drawing, terminal and wiring information, document register, factory-test scope, packing configuration, and any site modifications.
3. **Before shutdown mobilization:** confirm site survey results, lifting and transport arrangements, access permits, isolation responsibility, replacement materials, test resources, and emergency/temporary supply arrangements where the owner requires them.
4. **Before reconnection:** verify that the equipment installed is the approved equipment; close damage, storage, foundation, termination, earthing, accessory, and control-wiring issues with the responsible teams.
5. **Before energization:** use the project commissioning and switching process to confirm test results, protection and control readiness, latest drawings, permits, utility or AHJ release where required, and named authorization. The [pre-energization checklist](/resources/insights/transformer-pre-energization-checklist/) is a companion coordination tool.

No checklist can make a shutdown safe. Lockout/tagout, switching, grounding, lifting, oil handling, fire protection, testing, and energization must be planned and executed by qualified parties under the applicable regulations and site procedures.

## RFQ wording for a replacement project

> The bidder shall identify all information required to verify replacement of the existing transformer during the purchaser's planned outage. Submit the offered data sheet, outline/interface drawing, shipping dimensions, mass, centre of gravity, terminal and cable/bus connection details, auxiliary and alarm/trip terminal information, required factory records, packing configuration, and all exclusions. State every difference from the purchaser's approved system data and existing interface drawings. Site isolation, lifting, removal, disposal, cable or bus work, oil handling, civil modification, protection settings, field testing, switching, and energization are excluded unless expressly listed as included. Final suitability remains subject to the approved project documents, supplier manuals, site survey, applicable standards, local code, utility/AHJ requirements, and qualified engineering review.

## A practical decision rule

Commit to the shutdown only when the replacement is traceable to both sides of the interface: the approved transformer documents say what will arrive, and the verified site record says it can be removed, installed, connected, tested, and released. If either side is still based on an assumption, keep that item open and resolve it before it consumes the outage window.
