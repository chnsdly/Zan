Do not issue a transformer RFQ from a load figure alone when the project will connect to a public or private utility network. The buyer first needs a controlled statement of the connection boundary: what the network operator supplies, what the customer owns, and what has actually been approved at the point of connection. Until that is clear, a supplier may quote a technically plausible transformer that cannot be accepted, metered, protected, connected, or energized under the project process.

This guide helps factories, EPC teams, commercial developments and energy projects prepare that clarification. It is not a utility design, protection study, grounding design, switching instruction or permission to connect. The final arrangement must follow the connection offer, approved drawings, applicable market standards, local code, utility requirements and qualified engineering review.

## Treat the connection offer as an input, not a catalogue specification

The point of connection is more than a voltage label. In some projects it is a physical boundary at a cable termination; in others it is an operational, ownership or metering boundary that lies elsewhere. The responsible network operator normally defines what data and work it requires. For example, the UK regulator [Ofgem explains the connection process](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/information-consumers/energy-advice-households/getting-gas-or-electricity-connection) as involving the network operator, with separate arrangements for the meter and potentially for work carried out by an independent provider. That example is market-specific, not a global template.

Ask for the current, project-controlled connection offer or utility technical letter before comparing transformer quotations. Record the revision and its unresolved conditions. A preliminary capacity indication, an old single-line diagram, or a statement that the site has an "11 kV supply" does not by itself establish the required transformer ratio, connection arrangement, equipment ownership, fault-duty basis, metering location, protection interface or energization route.

For a power transformer, [IEC 60076-1](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/588) is a general transformer standard within its stated scope. It does not replace the utility's connection conditions or prescribe one universal customer-interface design. The applicable standard edition and project specification still need to be confirmed for the market.

## Build one boundary register before the RFQ is released

The most useful pre-RFQ document is a short register that gives each interface a source, owner and status. It keeps the supplier from silently filling a network-design gap with a default assumption.

| Interface | Evidence to obtain | RFQ question | Do not assume |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Point of connection and ownership | Connection offer, adopted single-line diagram, boundary drawing, ownership schedule | Where is the electrical, operational and asset-ownership boundary, and which equipment is in the supplier's scope? | That the transformer, HV cable, switchgear, meter cabinet or earthing components all belong to one party. |
| Supply characteristics | Utility letter or approved system study, including nominal voltage, frequency and any stated operating range | What supply data is confirmed, and which values remain subject to the utility's final issue? | That nominal voltage alone defines transformer ratio, tap requirement or insulation coordination. |
| Fault and protection interface | Utility-provided duty data where available, protection philosophy, approved single-line and relay responsibility matrix | Which data is supplied for design, and who approves protection settings, CT/VT use, intertrips and interlocks? | That a supplier can select or change system protection from a transformer rating alone. |
| Metering and communications | Metering arrangement, location, panel ownership, protocol or remote-control requirements where applicable | Is space, wiring, CT/VT provision, sealing, communications or a separate meter panel required? | That metering is inside the transformer supplier's scope or that a normal accessory terminal is an approved utility interface. |
| Site and civil interface | Utility layout conditions, approved plot plan, access route, foundation and cable-route drawings | What access, clearances, ducting, foundation, fire, drainage and handover constraints must the offered equipment meet? | That an outdoor package, plinth or cable entry can be chosen after the equipment is ordered. |
| Acceptance and energization | Required documents, witness points, commissioning plan, utility inspection and release process | Which submissions, tests, inspections and approvals are prerequisites to energization? | That factory records alone authorize connection or energization. |

The register is deliberately not a short-circuit calculation or a protection setting sheet. Those tasks require the responsible engineer and current system data. If the project needs to identify what data must flow from transformer to switchgear, use the [short-circuit-current and switchgear rating guide](/resources/insights/transformer-short-circuit-current-switchgear-rating/) as a companion question list, then obtain the project calculation and approvals.

## Separate confirmed data from bidder assumptions

A good RFQ asks bidders to identify their assumptions rather than rewarding the bid that hides them. Attach the approved information, label preliminary data clearly, and require a departures list. The offered transformer data sheet should trace its voltage ratio, tap arrangement, vector group, neutral arrangement, terminal interface, auxiliary supply and applicable standard basis back to named project documents.

This does not mean that every technical choice is fixed before quotation. It means that open choices have an owner. For example, a buyer may ask for options subject to utility confirmation, but the bid should state exactly what changes between those options: drawing interfaces, auxiliaries, dimensions, connection arrangement, testing records, price or programme. The [vector-group RFQ guide](/resources/insights/transformer-vector-group-rfq/) and [neutral-grounding RFQ guide](/resources/insights/transformer-neutral-grounding-rfq/) explain why neither item should be inferred from a familiar local practice.

When a package substation is being quoted, make the same boundary visible between the MV switchgear, transformer, LV board, meter provision, enclosure and site works. The [compact-substation interface checklist](/resources/insights/compact-substation-interface-rfq/) is useful for checking those package handoffs, but it cannot establish the utility's specific adoption or inspection rules.

## Questions that prevent a late connection redesign

Before award, assign a named answer owner to these questions:

1. Which drawing and connection-offer revision governs the electrical and physical boundary?
2. What is confirmed by the utility, what is provisional, and by when will outstanding data be issued?
3. Who owns and approves each HV interface, metering element, protective function, control point and communications connection?
4. Which equipment or works are utility-supplied, supplier-supplied, EPC-supplied or by others?
5. What evidence must be submitted before manufacture release, delivery, site acceptance and energization?

If any answer is "to be agreed on site," retain it as a controlled hold point rather than presenting it as a supplier obligation. This distinction matters when pricing is compared: a low price may simply exclude the equipment, drawing work, test support or coordination that another bidder identified.

## RFQ wording buyers can adapt

> The bidder shall base the offer on the purchaser's listed connection documents and identify every assumption, exclusion and departure. Submit a transformer or package-substation data sheet and interface drawing that identify the proposed voltage ratio, taps, vector group, neutral arrangement, terminal configuration, auxiliary requirements, dimensions, mass, applicable standard basis and document references. Do not infer utility approval from nominal system voltage.

> The bidder shall state the boundary of supply for transformer equipment, MV/LV switchgear where offered, metering provision, protection/control terminals, cable or bus interfaces, earthing connections, civil interfaces, factory records and site support. Utility equipment, protection settings, utility communications, field installation, testing, switching and energization are excluded unless expressly listed as included.

> Final suitability and release remain subject to the approved connection offer, project documents, supplier manuals and test records, applicable standards, local code, utility and AHJ requirements, and qualified engineering review.

## A practical release rule

Release the transformer RFQ only when every interface is either confirmed by a controlled document or marked as an open item with an owner, date and commercial treatment. Do not turn an uncertain utility boundary into an implied equipment feature. That small discipline makes quotations more comparable and gives the project a clear route to resolve utility, EPC and supplier responsibilities before the delivery schedule is at risk.
